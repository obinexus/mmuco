H-D1.1 — Ownership
The Hive garden shall be designed around a community-managed model in which the landowner retains legal title to the land.
Residents may participate in the management and maintenance of the Hive under a written governance and access agreement. Community management does not, by itself, transfer ownership, create a tenancy, or establish a separate legal entity.
Any formal trust, cooperative, lease, or other legal arrangement must be considered separately and approved before implementation.
H-D1.2 — Access
Access shall be available to residents and their authorised household members, subject to published rules covering safety, maintenance, ecological protection and responsible use of shared resources.
Access rules shall be applied consistently and lawfully. Restrictions must be based on legitimate requirements, not discriminatory criteria.
The Hive shall remain distinct from any wider public garden or public-access area unless a separate access decision is made.
H-D1.3 — Harvest and food allocation
The Hive shall use a shared-allocation model designed to support essential food needs.
The proposed principles are:

1. Essential needs first: prioritise access to harvested food according to a transparent, consistently applied allocation policy.
2. Shared production: coordinate communal crop beds and harvesting activities to support the resident community.
3. Transparent surplus distribution: record surplus and distribute it under published rules.
4. Reserve for future cycles: where appropriate, retain suitable seeds, compost inputs and other resources needed for continued production.
5. Traceability: maintain basic records of harvest quantities, distribution, losses and retained reserves.
6. Food safety: distribute only food considered suitable for consumption, using appropriate handling and storage practices.

No guaranteed food yield or level of self-sufficiency is claimed. These must be assessed through measured growing trials, seasonal records and realistic estimates of resident demand.
H-D1.4 — Governance and accountability
Before implementation, prepare a written agreement covering:

* Management responsibilities and maintenance schedules.
* Access authorisation and safety requirements.
* Harvest allocation and surplus distribution.
* Water use, crop management and ecological protection.
* Recordkeeping, complaints and dispute resolution.
* Costs, resource contributions and decision-making authority.
* Review, amendment and termination arrangements.

The agreement must be reviewed for compatibility with applicable law before it is relied upon as a binding legal arrangement.
H-D1.5 — Outstanding questions
H-D1 remains open for review until the project owner confirms:

* Who is the legal landowner?
* Who will coordinate day-to-day management?
* How will essential food needs and allocation priorities be assessed?
* How will residents participate in governance?
* What legal form, if any, should the management agreement take?
